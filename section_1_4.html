Additional exercises

Chapter 1 · Introduction

In this final section of the introductory chapter I collect a number of exercises in elementary ODE theory. Remember that “elementary” does not always mean “simple.”

Exercise 1.36. Sketch the integral curves of the equation \[ \dot x=\frac{t^2+x^2}{2}-1. \]

Exercise 1.37. Consider the equation \[ \dot x=\frac{x}{t}+\varphi\left(\frac{t}{x}\right). \] Find \(\varphi\) such that the general solution to this equation is given by \[ x(t)=\frac{t}{\log {\lvert Ct\rvert }}\,. \]

Exercise 1.38. Sketch the integral curves of \[ \sin \dot x=0. \]

Exercise 1.39. Prove that the differential equation for the second-order algebraic curves has the form \[ \left(\left(x''\right)^{-2/3}\right)'''=0. \]

Exercise 1.40. Prove that the equation \[ \dot x=2\sqrt[3]{tx} \] has more than one solution passing through the origin.

Exercise 1.41. Prove that all the solutions to \[ \dot x=\frac{1}{1+t^2+x^2} \] are bounded for all \(t\in\R\).

Exercise 1.42. Solve \[ x''-tx'-x=0. \]

Exercise 1.43. Prove that the equation \[ \dot x=x^2+t \] with the initial condition \(x(0)=0\) has no solution on the interval \([0,3)\).

Exercise 1.44. Let \(x\in \mathcal C^{(2)}(a,b)\cap \mathcal C[a,b]\) satisfy the equation \[ x''=e^t x \] and the boundary conditions \(x(a)=x(b)=0\). Find \(x\). How many solutions exist?

Exercise 1.45. Solve \[ \dot x=\max\{t,x\},\quad x(0)=0. \]
